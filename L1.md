# L1 — Risk-Driven Testing Assessment

## 1. Purpose

This procedure determines what evidence is required for a change, lifecycle transition, release, or continued-operation decision.

Lifecycle stage supplies a default confidence profile. It does not determine the test inventory. Evidence is selected from the claims being made, the architecture involved, the material failure modes, and the consequences of being wrong.

## 2. Entry triggers

Apply this procedure when:

* a material feature, refactor, bug fix, dependency, configuration, infrastructure, or deployment change is proposed,
* a project changes maturity or audience,
* a release or rollout decision is made,
* an incident, escaped defect, threat, performance shift, or usability finding changes the known risk profile,
* existing evidence becomes stale, unreliable, or non-comparable.

Low-risk routine work may use a lightweight record. High-impact, regulated, security-sensitive, or operationally complex work requires correspondingly stronger evidence and documentation.

## 3. Assessment state

Record the following as applicable:

### Decision context

* exploration,
* merge or feature completion,
* beta or limited rollout,
* production release,
* maintenance change,
* continued operation.

### Change context

* new behavior,
* refactor,
* defect correction,
* interface or schema change,
* dependency or platform change,
* operational configuration,
* deployment or scaling change,
* data or model change.

### System context

* users and operators,
* critical responsibilities and invariants,
* published or consumed interfaces,
* persistence and migration behavior,
* external services and infrastructure,
* supported environments,
* sensitive data and trust boundaries,
* latency, availability, recovery, and compliance obligations.

## 4. Build the risk and evidence map

For every material risk, record:

1. **Claim** — what must remain true.
2. **Failure mode** — how the claim could be false.
3. **Impact and likelihood** — including uncertainty where likelihood is unknown.
4. **Detection opportunity** — the earliest useful point at which the failure can be detected.
5. **Evidence** — tests, analysis, review, measurement, exploration, usability work, operational exercise, or production observation capable of detecting it.
6. **Evidence limits** — unrealistic doubles, missing environments, weak oracle, inadequate sample, untested recovery path, or other residual uncertainty.
7. **Decision rule** — what result passes, fails, or requires explicit acceptance.

Do not add a test class merely to fill a taxonomy. Do not omit evidence for a material risk merely because a lifecycle profile normally introduces it later.

## 5. Select structural scope, purpose, and technique

Use the independent dimensions defined in `Overview.md`.

* Select the smallest structural scope that preserves the semantics responsible for the risk.
* Add purpose labels such as acceptance, regression, contract, security, or performance independently of structural scope.
* Select techniques such as examples, properties, state machines, differential checks, fuzzing, snapshots, fault injection, exploration, or formal analysis according to the available oracle and input space.
* Select real dependencies, doubles, simulations, or production observations according to the required balance of fidelity and control.

## 6. Apply lifecycle confidence profiles

Use all applicable L2 profiles:

| Context | Default profile | Primary effect |
|---|---|---|
| Exploratory prototype | **L2-P1** | Reduce important uncertainty with minimal irreversible process cost. |
| Active feature development | **L2-P2** | Make changed responsibilities and acceptance conditions executable. |
| Stabilization or beta | **L2-P3** | Increase boundary, compatibility, user-journey, and suite-health evidence. |
| Production release | **L2-P4** | Require release, operational, recovery, and residual-risk evidence. |
| Live-system maintenance | **L2-P5** | Protect learned behavior and reassess changed boundaries and operations. |

Profiles may overlap. A production hotfix normally uses L2-P4 and L2-P5. A security-sensitive prototype may require evidence normally associated with production hardening.

## 7. Invoke L3 procedures

Invoke only the L3 procedures relevant to the selected evidence. Apply each procedure to the risks and claims in scope; do not mechanically apply every checklist item to every change.

The assessment must explain why omitted evidence is not material or how the residual risk is controlled. A formal waiver is required only when evidence that would otherwise be necessary for the decision is intentionally omitted.

## 8. Decision

Return one of:

* **finding** — exploratory result; no progression claim is made,
* **pass** — evidence supports the stated decision within recorded limits,
* **conditional pass** — evidence supports the decision only with explicit conditions, mitigations, or follow-up,
* **fail** — evidence does not support the stated decision.

A fail blocks only the decision whose claims are unsupported. It need not prohibit unrelated exploration or remediation work.

## 9. Record

Retain:

* decision and change context,
* risk and evidence map,
* relevant environments and artifact identities,
* results and suite-health status,
* residual uncertainty,
* waivers, owners, mitigations, and expiry or revisit triggers,
* final rationale.
