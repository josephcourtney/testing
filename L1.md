# Testing Assessment and Routing Procedure

## 1. Purpose

This procedure determines what evidence is needed for a change, release, deployment, or continued-operation decision.

It begins with the decision, claims, failure modes, architecture, and affected users. Lifecycle profiles adjust confidence and governance expectations; they do not decide whether an already-material risk applies.

## 2. Re-entry triggers

Apply this procedure when:

* a material change is proposed,
* a release or deployment decision is made,
* a lifecycle profile changes,
* a dependency, platform, configuration, data source, or operating environment changes materially,
* an incident, defect, user finding, or new threat changes the risk model,
* existing evidence becomes stale, non-comparable, unreliable, or incomplete.

## 3. Assessment record

Record the following.

### 3.1 Decision

State the decision to be made and its scope, such as exploration, merge, beta use, release, deployment, continued operation, or risk acceptance.

### 3.2 Claims

List what must be true for the decision to be justified.

### 3.3 Failure modes and consequences

For each material claim, identify plausible failure modes, affected users or operators, impact, detectability, reversibility, and exposure.

### 3.4 Architecture and boundaries

Identify relevant:

* public and supported interfaces,
* persistence and migration boundaries,
* processes, services, protocols, and platforms,
* configuration and feature variants,
* data sources and transformations,
* user and operator journeys,
* security, privacy, accessibility, performance, and recovery obligations.

### 3.5 Existing evidence

Inventory available evidence and state:

* which claim it supports,
* which failure mode it can detect,
* structural scope, purpose, technique, resources, and cadence,
* artifact, environment, data, and version identity,
* freshness, completeness, and comparability,
* known limitations.

### 3.6 Residual uncertainty

Record unsupported claims, untested conditions, weak or unrealistic doubles, missing environments, ambiguous requirements, and accepted risks.

## 4. Select evidence

For every material claim:

1. choose the least costly credible evidence that preserves the relevant semantics,
2. use multiple evidence forms when one form cannot establish the claim,
3. choose structural scope independently from purpose and technique,
4. include real boundary semantics where doubles are insufficient,
5. include human evaluation where judgment or user variation is intrinsic,
6. define measurement through L3-T11 before using a numeric gate,
7. define ownership, cadence, and evidence retention.

Do not require a named test class merely to complete an inventory. Record “not applicable” with rationale when a procedure does not correspond to a real claim or boundary.

## 5. Apply the lifecycle profile

Select the applicable profile:

* **L2-P1** — prototype,
* **L2-P2** — development / alpha,
* **L2-P3** — stabilization / beta,
* **L2-P4** — production,
* **L2-P5** — maintenance.

More than one profile may apply, such as a production hotfix during maintenance.

The profile may raise or lower default expectations for confidence, fidelity, breadth, cadence, enforcement, ownership, and recordkeeping. Risk overrides the profile in both directions.

## 6. Invoke L3 procedures

Apply every L3 procedure needed by the selected evidence. Common mappings include:

* structural scopes: L3-T1 through L3-T4,
* regression: L3-T5,
* generative techniques: L3-T6,
* contracts and compatibility: L3-T7,
* non-functional claims: L3-T8,
* snapshots: L3-T9,
* suite health: L3-T10,
* metrics: L3-T11,
* acceptance: L3-T12,
* exploration: L3-T13,
* usability and accessibility: L3-T14,
* operational resilience and recovery: L3-T15.

## 7. Decision

Classify the result as:

* **pass** — required claims have sufficient valid evidence,
* **conditional pass** — the decision may proceed with explicit conditions, mitigation, owner, and revisit trigger,
* **fail** — evidence contradicts a claim, is invalid, or leaves an unaccepted material gap,
* **exploratory finding** — useful learning was produced but no pass/fail decision is justified,
* **risk acceptance** — an authorized owner explicitly accepts a residual risk.

## 8. Required output

Retain:

* decision and lifecycle profile,
* claims and failure modes,
* architecture and boundary inventory,
* selected evidence and procedure references,
* results and evidence identity,
* residual uncertainty,
* waivers and risk acceptances,
* final decision and rationale.
