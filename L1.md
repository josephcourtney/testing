# Testing Assessment and Routing Procedure

## Level L1 — Project Lifecycle Testing Procedure

## 1. Purpose and scope

This procedure governs **which evidence is required** for a change, release,
deployment, continued-operation, or risk-acceptance decision and **which L2 and
L3 procedures must be invoked**.

It begins with the decision, claims, failure modes, architecture, affected users,
and existing evidence. Lifecycle profiles adjust required confidence, fidelity,
breadth, cadence, enforcement, ownership, and recordkeeping; they do not decide
whether an already-material risk applies.

This procedure does **not** define how to write individual tests, configure
specific tools, or evaluate a particular test technique. Those concerns are
delegated to the applicable L2 and L3 procedures and to non-normative
implementation guides.

## 2. Re-entry triggers

Apply this procedure continuously throughout the life of a project and re-enter
it whenever:

* the project changes lifecycle profile,
* a release, deployment, or continued-operation decision is made,
* a material feature, refactor, bug fix, dependency upgrade, infrastructure
  change, configuration change, or scaling change is proposed,
* externally visible behavior changes, including API responses, CLI output,
  persisted artifacts, reports, or user workflows,
* an integration boundary changes, including database, HTTP, message bus,
  filesystem, authentication, identity, secrets, configuration, or platform,
* data persistence, schema, migration, retention, or lineage semantics change,
* security-, privacy-, accessibility-, performance-, availability-, recovery-,
  data-, or correctness-critical behavior changes,
* a runtime or platform dependency introduces non-trivial behavioral risk,
* an incident, escaped defect, user finding, new threat, or operating observation
  changes the risk model,
* required evidence becomes stale, non-comparable, incomplete, or unreliable.

A change is material when it could invalidate a claim needed for the decision,
even if the code diff is small.

## 3. Assessment state

Determine and record the following.

### 3.1 Decision

State the decision being made and its scope, for example:

* exploration or prototype continuation,
* merge,
* internal or beta use,
* production release,
* deployment or staged rollout,
* continued operation,
* explicit risk acceptance.

### 3.2 Lifecycle profile

Record one or more applicable profiles:

* `prototype` — L2-P1,
* `alpha` or `development` — L2-P2,
* `beta` or `stabilization` — L2-P3,
* `production` — L2-P4,
* `maintenance` — L2-P5.

A production hotfix, for example, may require both the production and
maintenance profiles.

### 3.3 Change context

Record relevant contexts such as:

* new feature,
* refactor,
* bug fix,
* dependency upgrade,
* operational configuration,
* deployment,
* migration,
* scaling,
* incident response.

### 3.4 Claims

List what must be true for the decision to be justified. Include product,
technical, operational, security, privacy, data, accessibility, usability, and
compatibility claims where material.

### 3.5 Failure modes and consequences

For each material claim, identify:

* plausible ways the claim could be false,
* affected users, operators, consumers, or data,
* likelihood or exposure where useful,
* impact and reversibility,
* detectability,
* available mitigation and rollback.

### 3.6 Architecture and boundaries

Identify relevant:

* public and supported interfaces,
* modules and coherent components,
* persistence, schema, and migration boundaries,
* processes, services, protocols, brokers, and platforms,
* configuration and feature variants,
* data sources, transformations, and outputs,
* user and operator journeys,
* deployment, monitoring, recovery, and rollback paths.

### 3.7 Existing evidence

For each relevant evidence source, record:

* the claim and failure mode it addresses,
* structural scope, purpose, technique, resource use, and cadence,
* artifact, revision, environment, data, dependency, and configuration identity,
* whether the result is complete or partial,
* freshness and comparability,
* known limitations, unvalidated doubles, and unsupported conditions.

### 3.8 Residual uncertainty

Record unsupported claims, missing environments, ambiguous requirements,
unrealistic substitutes, insufficient samples, unexplored areas, stale evidence,
and risks accepted rather than eliminated.

## 4. Select evidence from risk and architecture

For every material claim:

1. Choose the least costly credible evidence that preserves the relevant
   semantics.
2. Use more than one evidence form when one form cannot establish the claim.
3. Choose structural scope independently from purpose, technique, resource use,
   and cadence.
4. Include real boundary semantics where doubles are insufficient.
5. Include human evaluation where judgment, comprehension, accessibility, or
   user variation is intrinsic.
6. Apply L3-T11 before using a quantitative measurement as a target or gate.
7. Define ownership, cadence, retention, and response to failure.

Do not require a named test class merely to complete an inventory. A procedure
may be marked **not applicable** only with a rationale tied to the product's
actual claims and boundaries.

## 5. Lifecycle routing

After the risk-and-evidence assessment, apply the relevant L2 profile or
profiles.

| Lifecycle profile | Primary intent | Required L2 procedure | Default governance emphasis |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Prototype** | Explore feasibility and reduce uncertainty | **L2-P1: Exploratory / Prototype** | Minimal ceremony; explicit assumptions and material-risk controls |
| **Alpha / Development** | Establish changed behavior while iterating rapidly | **L2-P2: Feature Development** | Executable changed responsibilities and fast diagnostic feedback |
| **Beta / Stabilization** | Stabilize behavior and reduce release uncertainty | **L2-P3: Stabilization and Pre-Release** | Critical boundaries, compatibility, user journeys, and suite health |
| **Production** | Establish release and operational readiness | **L2-P4: Release and Hardening** | Enforced, fresh, comparable evidence and explicit residual risk |
| **Maintenance** | Safely evolve a live system | **L2-P5: Maintenance and Change** | Change impact, incident learning, evidence decay, and continued operation |

Risk overrides these defaults in both directions. A prototype handling sensitive
data may require production-strength security or privacy evidence. A mature local
utility without an external service boundary does not need fictitious service
integration tests.

## 6. Invoke L3 procedures

Apply every L3 procedure needed by the selected evidence:

* **L3-T1** — unit testing,
* **L3-T2** — component testing,
* **L3-T3** — integration testing,
* **L3-T4** — system testing,
* **L3-T5** — regression testing,
* **L3-T6** — generative, property, model, and differential testing,
* **L3-T7** — contract and compatibility testing,
* **L3-T8** — non-functional testing,
* **L3-T9** — snapshot and golden testing,
* **L3-T10** — test-suite health,
* **L3-T11** — metric design and validation,
* **L3-T12** — acceptance testing,
* **L3-T13** — exploratory testing,
* **L3-T14** — usability and accessibility testing,
* **L3-T15** — operational, resilience, and recovery testing.

Multiple procedures commonly apply to one item of evidence. A system-scoped
contract regression using a snapshot, for example, may invoke T4, T5, T7, and
T9.

## 7. Process

For each applicable L2 and L3 procedure:

1. Execute the procedure or document why it is not applicable.
2. Record selected evidence, commands, artifacts, versions, environments, and
   limitations.
3. Identify unmet requirements, waivers, and accepted risks.
4. Assign owners and revisit triggers to unresolved items.
5. Return the procedure outcome to this L1 assessment.

Do not allow a later lifecycle profile to conceal an unresolved risk identified
by an earlier assessment.

## 8. Aggregation and decision rules

Aggregate procedure outcomes by claim rather than by test count.

* **Pass** — every required claim has sufficient valid evidence for the stated
  decision, with no unresolved blocking gap.
* **Conditional pass** — the decision may proceed only with explicit conditions,
  mitigations, owners, and revisit triggers.
* **Fail** — evidence contradicts a required claim, is invalid, or leaves an
  unaccepted material gap.
* **Exploratory finding** — useful learning was produced, but the evidence does
  not justify a pass/fail decision.
* **Risk acceptance** — an authorized owner explicitly accepts a residual risk
  with rationale, mitigation, and review conditions.

A passing low-scope suite does not compensate for missing evidence about a
material boundary, user journey, operational claim, or unsupported environment.

## 9. Required output

Retain:

* decision and lifecycle profile,
* change context,
* claims and failure modes,
* affected users, operators, consumers, and data,
* architecture and boundary inventory,
* selected evidence and procedure references,
* artifact, environment, version, and configuration identity,
* results and measurement conditions,
* residual uncertainty,
* waivers and risk acceptances,
* final decision and rationale.
